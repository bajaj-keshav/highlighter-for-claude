Explain the Krebs cycle for my biology exam.

The Krebs cycle (also called the citric acid cycle) is the central step of cellular respiration. It takes place in the mitochondrial matrix and turns acetyl-CoA into carbon dioxide while it captures energy.

Key steps

  1. Acetyl-CoA joins oxaloacetate to make citrate.
  2. Citrate changes to isocitrate.
  3. Isocitrate is oxidized to α-ketoglutarate, and one CO₂ is released.
  4. α-Ketoglutarate is oxidized to succinyl-CoA, and a second CO₂ is released.

What one turn produces

Each turn makes 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 GTP (or ATP) and 2 CO₂. Because one glucose gives two acetyl-CoA, the cycle turns twice for each glucose.

Remember: the cycle itself makes little ATP directly. Its main job is to load NADH and FADH₂ with electrons for the electron transport chain.
